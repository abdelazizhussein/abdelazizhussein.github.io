Abstract

We build a theoretical range for the Milky Way's inner dark matter distribution informed by the FIRE-2, Auriga, VINTERGATAN-GM, and TNG50 simulation suites, assuming the canonical cold dark matter (CDM) model. This work provides crucial constraints for direct detection experiments and indirect dark matter searches by establishing robust predictions for the local dark matter density profile.

Physics & Methodology

The dark matter density profiles in Auriga, VINTERGATAN-GM, and TNG50 simulations can be approximately modeled using the adiabatic contraction prescription of Gnedin et al. (2004), while FIRE-2 exhibits stronger baryonic feedback, leading to departures from the adiabatic contraction model. The simulated halos that undergo adiabatic contraction are close to spherical with axis ratios q ∈ [0.75-0.9] at 5 kpc.
ρ(r) = ρ₀ / [(r/rₛ)(1 + r/rₛ)²]     (NFW Profile)
We analyze the impact of baryonic physics on the dark matter distribution, particularly examining how stellar feedback and gravitational interactions modify the expected density profiles from dark matter-only simulations.

Research Experience & Impact

This research combines theoretical modeling with cutting-edge cosmological simulations to understand how baryonic physics affects dark matter distributions in Milky Way-like galaxies. The work directly informs experimental searches for dark matter particles.
  • Theoretical Challenge: Reconciling different simulation predictions for inner galaxy dark matter profiles and understanding the role of baryonic feedback mechanisms.
  • Computational Methods: Analysis of multiple state-of-the-art cosmological simulation suites (FIRE-2, Auriga, VINTERGATAN-GM, TNG50) to establish robust theoretical predictions.
  • Experimental Relevance: Results provide critical input for direct detection experiments and constrain local dark matter density for particle physics searches.

Stellar Feedback in Dark Matter Halos

Simulation Code
Draft Paper

Overview

Investigation of how stellar feedback mechanisms - including supernovae, stellar winds, and radiation pressure - affect the distribution and dynamics of dark matter in galactic halos. This work explores the complex interplay between baryonic and dark matter components in galaxy formation.

Physical Processes

We study energy injection from stellar processes and its impact on dark matter halo structure. Key mechanisms include supernova explosions that can create cavities in the dark matter distribution, and stellar winds that provide continuous energy input affecting the gravitational potential.
E_SN ≈ 10⁵¹ erg     (Typical supernova energy)
The cumulative effect of stellar feedback can lead to significant modifications of the inner dark matter profile, particularly in lower-mass halos where the escape velocity is comparable to stellar wind velocities.

Research Experience

This project involves both analytical modeling of stellar feedback processes and numerical simulations to track their effects on dark matter distributions over cosmic time.
  • Physics Modeling: Developed semi-analytical models for stellar feedback efficiency as a function of halo mass and stellar population characteristics.
  • Computational Skills: Implemented custom feedback prescriptions in hydrodynamical simulation codes and analyzed multi-scale physics interactions.
  • Astrophysical Insights: Quantified how stellar feedback creates diversity in dark matter halo profiles, explaining observed galaxy-to-galaxy variations.

Research Motivation

Exploring alternatives to dark matter by investigating modified gravity theories and their predictions for galactic rotation curves. This work examines whether modifications to Einstein's general relativity can explain observed galactic dynamics without invoking dark matter.

Theoretical Framework

We analyze orbital dynamics in modified gravity theories such as MOND (Modified Newtonian Dynamics) and f(R) gravity. The key insight is that at low accelerations (a < a₀ ≈ 10⁻¹⁰ m/s²), the gravitational force law may deviate from Newton's inverse square law.
